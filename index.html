THE DREAM WAS ALREADY THERE

A real beginning.

Before there was a website or a flight plan, there were airport windows, aircraft exhibits and the unmistakable excitement of getting close to airplanes.

We're not pretending to know exactly where this ends. We're taking seriously where it begins.

This isn't the story of a pilot yet. It's the story of becoming one.

THE PURPOSE

Learn to fly. Be ready.
Go where help is needed.

Aviation can become much more than a license. Ariana may someday choose wildfire aviation, emergency response, humanitarian flying, another aviation career — or something none of us can predict today.

The destination is hers to choose. The opportunity to prepare is something we can build now.

THE FLIGHT PLAN

A runway, not a deadline.

Every stage should create skills and evidence before asking for the next investment.

12

Explore

Young Eagles, aviation education, airport exposure, mentors and simulator fundamentals.

13

Build

Ground-school learning, first formal lesson opportunities and a documented aviation log.

14–15

Prepare

Explore glider pathways, deepen ground knowledge and build a scholarship-ready record.

16

Solo

If she chooses and is ready, work toward airplane student-pilot milestones and first solo.

17+

Earn

Work toward a private pilot certificate and decide whether professional training is next.

18+

Serve

Instrument, commercial and specialized experience if aviation becomes her calling.

FAA rules allow flying lessons before a student pilot certificate is required; the certificate is needed before solo flight.

FUND THE PATH SMARTER

Not one fundraiser.
A funding stack.

The plan is to reduce what the family has to pay by combining free youth programs, earned scholarships, donated opportunities, sponsorships and direct family/community support.

START NOW · FREE

EAA Young Eagles

Ages 8–17 can receive a free introductory flight. After a Young Eagles flight, participants can unlock free EAA student membership and aviation-learning resources.

EXPLORE YOUNG EAGLES ↗
LEARN · FREE

Sporty's Learn to Fly

Young Eagles can access the online Learn to Fly course free. Progress can lead to a free first flight lesson when eligible and reimbursement after passing the FAA knowledge exam.

AGE 16–18 · SCHOLARSHIP

AOPA Foundation

AOPA's current program includes at least 90 scholarships of $12,000 for eligible high-school students ages 16–18. The next application period opens October 1, 2026.

ONGOING

Scholarship portfolio

Build the grades, training log, service record, recommendations and aviation experience that future scholarship applications can actually use.

LOCAL AVIATION

Donated opportunity

Invite pilots, flight schools, aviation businesses and organizations to contribute access: mentorship, simulator time, tours, ground instruction or flight experiences.

SPONSORSHIP

Fund a milestone

Instead of vague donations, sponsors can eventually support specific verified milestones — a course, exam, lesson block, equipment need or training stage.

THE RULE:Use free first. Earn what can be earned. Ask for help where help creates the next real milestone.

FROM DAD

My job isn't to decide where she lands.

I can't become a pilot for her. I can't know whether she'll want the same thing at 16, 18 or 25. But I can take seriously what she's telling me at 12. I can find opportunities. I can put good people around her. I can help her start.

My job is to help make sure she has a runway.

BECOME PART OF THE RUNWAY

FUTURE SPONSORS

Funding transparency

This project will distinguish scholarships, donated experiences, sponsorships and personal/community support. No contribution will be described as tax-deductible unless it is processed through a qualifying charitable organization or fiscal sponsor. Training costs will be published only when verified.
