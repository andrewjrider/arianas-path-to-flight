HER STORY

This isn't the story of a pilot yet.
It's the story of becoming one.

Long before there was a website, a fundraiser or a flight plan, there were airport windows. Airplanes to stop and look at. Aviation exhibits to climb into. Family trips where the aircraft mattered almost as much as the destination.

Those moments are part of Ariana's real family archive. Now, at 12, that curiosity has become something more deliberate: she wants to learn to fly.

01

WHY FLY?

Because flying can become a way to help.

Ariana isn't being asked to choose her entire future at twelve. She doesn't need to know the exact aircraft, job or mission yet.

What matters is the reason she wants to begin: to build a skill that could someday make her useful when someone needs help.

Wildfire aviation. Emergency response. Humanitarian work. Or a mission she hasn't discovered yet.

THE IDEA

12 is not
too young.

02

START WHERE YOU ARE

Use these years well.

At 12, the goal isn't to rush toward a certificate. It's to build the foundation: introductory flying, aviation education, ground-school knowledge, mentors, simulator experience and time around people who know what good flying looks like.

Every step should answer one question: does Ariana still want the next one?

THE FLIGHT PLAN

A runway, not a deadline.

The plan grows with Ariana. Each stage creates the opportunity for the next without pretending we already know where she'll land.

12

Start

Introductory flying, aviation education, mentors and exploration.

13

Build

Ground knowledge, simulator work, STEM and consistent exposure.

14–15

Explore more

Glider opportunities, deeper ground school and scholarship preparation.

16

Solo

When ready and eligible, work toward the first airplane solo.

17+

Earn the wings

Private pilot certification, then decide what comes next.

18+

Find her mission

Advanced training and the long road toward service aviation if she chooses it.

FROM DAD

“My job isn't to decide where Ariana lands.”

I can't become a pilot for her. I can't know whether she'll want exactly the same thing at 16, 18 or 25. But I can take seriously what she's telling me at 12. I can find opportunities. I can put good people around her. I can help her start.

“My job is to help make sure she has a runway.”
